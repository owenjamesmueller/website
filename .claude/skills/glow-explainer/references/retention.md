# Retention & emotional resonance: make them watch to the end

Every script and every edit must pass this checklist before voice is generated and before the final
render. Sources are practitioner research (hook timing, open loops, silent viewing, endings) plus what
the reference videos already do. Treat the numbers as rules of thumb, not law.

## 1. The first frame and the first 2 seconds (the scroll-stop)
- **Frame 0 is the thumbnail.** Characters already on stage, plus a 2–5 word text hook. Never start on an empty floor.
- **Layered hook**: the picture, the spoken line and the on-screen text all say the same paradox at once.
  Many viewers start muted, so the text alone must make sense ("SAME JOB. SAME PAY." → "20 YEARS LATER: ONLY ONE IS RICH.").
- Something moves within 0.3 s. Spoken hook ≤ 3 s, no intro and no "hey guys".

## 2. Open loops (why they keep watching)
- Open a question in the first 3 s and **don't answer it until the end**. Show *that* something happens, hide *who/how*
  ("Guess which one?" with a spotlight swinging between the two characters).
- Stack 3–4 loops in a 60 s video, closing the newest first: the hook question → "the crazy part is…" → "but here's
  what nobody saw" → the callback. Each closing should open the next.
- Mid-video re-hook at about 40–50 %: a spoken turn ("But here's what nobody saw.") plus a visual pattern interrupt
  (everything fades except one character, the camera pushes in, a bass hit).

## 3. Emotional beats (what makes it resonate and get shared)
Hit at least five of these; the script audit below scores them.
| beat | what it looks like | why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Relatable identity** | "two best friends", "the smartest kid from *your* school" | viewer maps it onto someone they know |
| **Unfairness / mystery** | "they earned almost exactly the same money" | removes the easy explanation → curiosity |
| **Underdog humiliation** | everyone laughs; light narrows to him alone; heartbeat | viewer picks a side, needs to see him vindicated |
| **Temptation / envy** | "for years it looked like Marcus was winning" | honest about why the wrong path feels right |
| **Hidden effort** | nights alone, terrible at it for a year | earns the payoff; makes success feel deserved |
| **The first win** | a stranger pays him; shake + ding + arms up | dopamine spike at ~55 % to carry into the second half |
| **Contrast / fear** | the leaking bucket; "still needs every paycheck" | quiet regret: "is that me?" (drives saves and shares) |
| **Vindication** | "doesn't need his at all": punch-in, big tree, chord | release of the tension built by the humiliation |
| **Twist callback** | "it happened on that very first payday, while everyone was laughing" | recolours the whole video, so people rewatch |
| **Quotable rule** | "Marcus got paid by his job. Jake kept buying things that paid him." | the line people screenshot and comment |

## 4. Pacing (pattern interrupts)
- Fast mode: ~210 wpm, gaps ≤ 0.1 s, `GX_PACE 0.72`, `punches()` on every sentence.
- A new visual event at least every 1.5 s and a scene change at least every 4–5 s. Sound accents on scene changes.
- Never hold a static frame. The camera always drifts.

## 5. The ending (completion → rewatch → comment)
- The last line pays off the first loop *and* recontextualises it (twist callback).
- Title card: 2–3 words that turn the moral into an instruction ("PLANT IT EARLY."), held ≤ 2 s, then cut.
  Don't fade out slowly: viewers swipe during long outros.
- Comment bait built into the story, not asked for: "Guess which one?", or a quotable rule people will argue with.

## Script audit (score before spending voice credits; ship ≥ 8/10)
1. Hook is a paradox ≤ 3 s spoken, and works as muted text.  2. An open loop stays unanswered until the last 15 %.
3. A specific relatable character (named, or "your…").  4. A concrete micro-moment within 10 s (shoes, laughing).
5. An underdog or injustice beat.  6. A spoken turn mid-video ("But…").  7. A first-win spike around 50–60 %.
8. A quotable rule.  9. Consequence/fear for the other path.  10. A twist callback ending + an instruction title card.
